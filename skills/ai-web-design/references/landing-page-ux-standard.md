# Landing page UX standard

> Used by path 3 (PPC) to propose the structure and to review the finished
> page. Prevents typical mistakes — it doesn't guarantee a perfect page;
> what works best for a specific campaign shows only in measurement and
> A/B tests after launch.

## First screen (visible without scrolling)
- Headline repeats the promise of the ad the person clicked.
- Subheadline: one sentence on what exactly they get and who it's for.
- Main CTA visible without scrolling, on mobile too.
- One visual showing the product or result, not decoration.
- Short proof right at the CTA (number of clients, rating, logos).

## One goal
- One main action on the whole page, repeated after key sections.
- Same CTA text everywhere, saying what happens ("Get a free consultation",
  not "Submit").
- No menu or links leading off the page. Exceptions: unlinked logo, legal
  links in the footer.
- Secondary action (e.g. phone) only if the campaign brief asks for it.

## Section order (baseline, adjust per campaign)
1. Hero with promise + CTA
2. The problem the audience has
3. Solution + 3 main benefits
4. How it works (3–4 steps)
5. Proof (references, numbers, case studies)
6. Objections / FAQ
7. Final CTA with the form
Shorter for warm audiences (retargeting), longer for expensive/complex
products.

## Form
- Only fields sales really needs for first contact — every extra field
  lowers submissions.
- Labels above fields, not just grey placeholder text.
- Errors shown immediately at the specific field, in plain language.
- Form notice under the button; marketing consent separate.
- After submit: clear confirmation of what happens next and when.

## Trust
- Proof must be real and verifiable, never invented.
- References with name, company or photo if the client allows.
- Visible contact details + company identification in the footer.

## Copy
- Customer benefits, not a feature list.
- Concrete numbers instead of generic phrases.
- Short paragraphs; headings that make sense when skimming.

## Mobile + speed
- Designed mobile-first — campaign traffic is often mostly mobile.
- Buttons large enough for a finger.
- Fast load: compressed images, no unnecessary scripts.

## Measurement + testing
- Conversions tracked per the campaign brief (GA4, Google Ads, Meta); UTM
  parameters stored with the lead.
- In an A/B test change one thing at a time (e.g. the headline).

## Accessibility + legal
- Same minimum as every site: contrast, keyboard, labels, cookie bar,
  legal links.
